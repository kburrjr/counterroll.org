Read the Introduction

by Aaron Theophilus

COUNTER-ROLL PUBLISHING

The fourth volume closed with the generations of Esau disposed of in their roll and Jacob left alone at the threshold of the tenth toledoth, the generations of Jacob, upon which the whole book of Genesis turns at last. The present volume follows that toledoth to its end. It is the volume of Joseph, and it is the volume of the ledger. Here the seed is sold into the foreign jurisdiction, the dreams and the pit open the longest movement of the book, and the exile pattern is set: the covenant people preserved inside the house of their captors, the steward raised to the right hand of the throne by the very faithfulness that the pit could not bury.

Its span is the whole administration of the foreign house. Judah and Tamar carry the Messiah's line through the breach; Joseph refuses in Potiphar's house and suffers the false accusation; the butler and the baker wait out the appointed time in prison. Pharaoh's dreams are read, and the ledger doctrine enters the record: the twenty percent and the storehouses of plenty, the corn gathered against the seven years of famine as the nation's reserve. The brethren come down and are tested, the money returned in the sacks, Judah's pledge given as surety for Benjamin, the cup found in the sack, and the great intercession made. Joseph reveals himself, the hidden administration is declared, and the seed migrates into Goshen, preserved within the foreign jurisdiction. The land is bought into the crown for Pharaoh and leased back at the fixed fifth, the pattern of the settlement. Jacob blesses the sons of Joseph with the crossed hands, the younger set before the elder, and gathers the twelve tribes about his bed to declare each portion in order before he dies and is carried back to the cave of Machpelah. The book closes as it must: with the charge of Joseph concerning his bones, and the coffin in Egypt, the seed held in trust against the exodus, the covenant people waiting in the foreign land for the deliverance that the next book of the Blueprint will open.

The doctrine carried in this volume is the law of the fiduciary administration at national scale: the faithful steward in the foreign house, the twenty percent as the fixed portion laid up against the famine, the surety of the brother offered for the brother, the land-into-crown purchase and leaseback, the crossed hands of the testamentary blessing, and the coffin as the covenant, the pledge that the seed would not be left in Egypt.
